Most brand-visibility guides still talk only about ChatGPT and Gemini. DeepSeek deserves a place in the same routine. It is a widely used AI assistant, it answers product and vendor questions directly, and its answers shape shortlists before a buyer ever visits your website. This guide explains how to check your DeepSeek brand visibility, what the results mean, and how to improve them.

## How to test what DeepSeek says about you

Start with ten prompts a real buyer would type. Mix branded and unbranded questions:

- "What is [your brand]?"
- "Best [category] tools for small businesses"
- "[Your brand] vs [competitor]"
- "How much does [your category] software cost?"
- "Which [category] tool is easiest to set up?"

Run each prompt in a fresh DeepSeek chat, and record four things for every answer: whether your brand is mentioned, where it appears in the list, whether the description is accurate, and which sources or citations the answer points to. A spreadsheet with one row per prompt is enough. Repeat the same prompts weekly, because AI answers change over time.

## What to look for in the results

Four patterns tell you most of what you need to know.

**No mention.** Your brand never appears for unbranded category prompts. This is the most common result for small brands, and it is fixable: it usually means there is too little clear, factual content about you for the model to retrieve and trust.

**Mentioned, but late.** You appear fifth or lower, after bigger competitors. Buyers rarely read that far. Your goal is to move up by being the clearest answer to the question, not by stuffing keywords.

**Mentioned, but wrong.** The answer describes an old price, a discontinued feature, or a different company. This happens when outdated pages still rank as sources. Fix the source pages first; the model can only repeat what it finds.

**Mentioned, with citations.** The answer links to your site, your docs, or a reputable third-party page about you. Citations are the strongest signal: they mean the model found something worth pointing to, and they give readers a path to verify the claim.

## How to improve your DeepSeek visibility

There is no shortcut and no paid placement inside a model's answer. What works is the same discipline that works for other AI models:

1. **Say plainly what you do.** Your homepage should state your product, audience, and price in the first screen, in simple sentences a model can quote accurately.
2. **Publish factual pages, not slogans.** Pricing, feature, comparison, and documentation pages give models quotable facts. Vague marketing copy does not.
3. **Keep facts current.** Update prices, plans, and features the day they change, so outdated pages stop being the source of wrong answers.
4. **Earn third-party mentions.** Directory listings, reviews, and honest comparisons from other sites are independent sources a model can cite alongside your own pages.
5. **Answer the exact questions buyers ask.** A short page titled with the buyer's question, answered directly in the first paragraph, is far more quotable than a long essay that buries the answer.

Then re-run your ten prompts after a few weeks. Visibility work is slow: expect movement over weeks, not overnight, and judge it by the trend across all ten prompts, not by a single lucky answer.

## A simple weekly routine

Set aside thirty minutes a week. Run your prompt set, log mentions, position, accuracy, and citations, and note one fix: one outdated page to update, one missing comparison to publish, or one question your site never answers. Over a quarter, that routine produces a visible trend line and a clear backlog of content worth writing.
