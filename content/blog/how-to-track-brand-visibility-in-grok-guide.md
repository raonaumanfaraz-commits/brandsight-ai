Most brand visibility guides focus on ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini. Grok, the AI assistant from xAI, deserves a place in the same routine. It is built into X, it answers with a strong real-time flavour, and it draws on live posts and web sources. If your customers spend time on X, Grok is one of the places they will ask for recommendations.

## Start with the questions your customers actually ask

AI visibility tracking starts with prompts, not with the tool. Write down the questions a real buyer would ask. For a project management tool, that might be "what is the best project management tool for a small remote team" or "which project management software has the best free plan". Add comparison prompts like "Tool A vs Tool B for agencies" and problem prompts like "how do I keep a remote team on schedule". Ten to twenty prompts is a good starting set, and they should cover your category, your competitors, and the problem you solve, not just your brand name.

## Ask Grok the same prompts, the same way, every week

Open Grok and run each prompt in a fresh conversation. Record three things for every prompt: whether your brand is mentioned at all, where it appears in the answer (first, middle, or an afterthought), and what it is linked to. Grok answers often cite X posts and web pages, so note which sources appear alongside your brand. Run the same prompts again the following week and compare. One snapshot tells you little; the trend over four to six weeks tells you whether your visibility is growing, flat, or slipping.

## Read the answer like a buyer would

A mention is not the same as a recommendation. Grok may name your brand and still recommend a competitor, or mention you only in a list of alternatives. For each prompt, ask yourself whether a buyer reading that answer would shortlist you. Note the words Grok uses about your brand. Is it described as affordable, easy to use, or enterprise-grade? Does it attach a strength or a weakness to you? That framing is the reputation AI search is building for you, and it matters as much as the mention itself.

## Track your competitors in the same prompts

Run the same prompt set with your competitors in mind. When Grok recommends a competitor, read why. Often the answer will cite a specific source: a review page, a comparison article, a popular X thread, or a help doc. Those citations are your to-do list. If a competitor keeps winning because of a detailed comparison page or a well-ranked review, that is the gap to close.

## Watch the real-time angle

Grok's edge is real-time information. It is more likely than other assistants to surface fresh X discussions, breaking news, and recent launches. That cuts both ways. A positive viral thread about your product can lift your visibility quickly, and a public complaint can do the opposite. When you check Grok weekly, also scan what is being said about your brand on X that week. If something new is trending, Grok will probably reflect it before other assistants do.

## Turn the findings into fixes

Tracking only pays off when it changes what you publish. If Grok never mentions you for a key prompt, the fix is usually content: a clear comparison page, a use-case page, or a help article that answers that exact question. If Grok mentions you but frames you wrongly, fix the source it cites, whether that is your own site or a third-party page. If a competitor keeps winning a prompt, study the cited source and build something better. Keep a simple log of prompt, mention, position, and cited source, and review it monthly to decide what to write next.
