Perplexity has become a quiet force in how people research products. Instead of a page of blue links, it gives a written answer with cited sources. When a buyer asks Perplexity for the best tool in your category, your brand either appears in that answer or it is invisible at the exact moment a decision is forming.

## Why Perplexity deserves its own tracking

Perplexity works differently from a chatbot. Every answer cites the pages it drew from, so a mention is always tied to a source you can inspect. That makes Perplexity visibility unusually actionable: if a competitor is cited and you are not, you can open the cited page and see exactly what earned the mention.

It also reaches a different audience. Perplexity users tend to ask detailed, comparison-heavy questions, such as which tool is best for a specific team size or budget. Those are late-stage buying questions, so a mention there is worth more than a passing reference in a general chat answer.

## Build a prompt list first

Start by writing down the questions your buyers actually ask. Cover four types of prompt. Branded prompts ask about you directly, such as "Is BrandSight worth it for agencies?" Category prompts ask for the best option, such as "best AI brand monitoring tool for small teams." Comparison prompts pit you against a rival, such as "BrandSight vs Brand24 for startups." Problem prompts describe a need without naming any brand, such as "how do I know if ChatGPT mentions my company?"

Aim for 20 to 30 prompts to start. Fewer than that and a single answer change swings your numbers wildly. More than that and the weekly check becomes a chore you will abandon.

## Check the answers the same way every week

Perplexity answers change as its sources update, so consistency matters more than any single result. Pick one day a week, run every prompt in a fresh session, and record three things for each answer: whether your brand is mentioned, where in the answer it appears, and which sources are cited.

Keep a simple spreadsheet with one row per prompt and one column per week. Mark each week as mentioned, not mentioned or cited as a source. Over a month you will see a trend line, and trend lines are what tell you whether your content work is paying off.

## Read the citations, not just the mentions

A mention without a citation is weaker than a mention with one, because Perplexity users click the sources. When your brand is mentioned, note which page was cited. When a competitor is cited, open that page and see exactly what earned the mention.

If your own pages are rarely cited, the fix is usually content, not promotion. Write pages that answer the exact question in the prompt, use clear headings, include concrete numbers and facts, and keep the page updated. Perplexity favors sources that answer directly and look current.

## Turn the data into action

Review your sheet once a month and sort prompts into three groups. Strong prompts are ones where you appear and are cited. Weak prompts are ones where you appear without a citation. Missing prompts are ones where you never appear at all.

For weak prompts, strengthen the page that should be cited: add the facts Perplexity quoted from competitors and tighten the answer. For missing prompts, check whether any page on your site answers that question at all. If not, write one.
