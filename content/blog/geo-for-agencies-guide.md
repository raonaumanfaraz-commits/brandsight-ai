Your clients are starting to ask a new question. It is no longer just "where do we rank on Google" but "what does ChatGPT say about us?" That question is the opening for a service most small agencies can sell today: GEO, or generative engine optimization. This guide explains what the service looks like, how to price it, and how to report on it without building new infrastructure.

## What clients actually want

Clients do not buy "GEO" as a concept. They buy an answer to a practical worry: when a potential customer asks an AI assistant for a recommendation in our category, does our brand come up? If it does, are we recommended, mentioned in passing, or listed after three competitors? If it does not, what would change that?

An agency GEO service answers those questions with a regular check. You run a fixed set of prompts about the client and its category through ChatGPT, Gemini, and DeepSeek, then record whether the client is mentioned, where it appears in the answer, and which sources get cited. That is the whole measurement. Everything else, the report, the recommendations, the retainer, hangs off it.

## Start with a free check, not a pitch deck

The fastest way to open the conversation is to run the check before you pitch. Pick a prospect, run its brand through a free AI visibility check, and send the result with one short message: here is what AI assistants currently say about you, and here is the gap. A prospect that sees its own brand missing from answers understands the service in thirty seconds. Do the same for existing clients: a monthly visibility report added to a current SEO retainer is an easier sale than a brand-new service line.

## What to include in the monthly service

Keep the deliverable small enough to repeat every month. A workable package has four parts. First, the tracking itself: a fixed prompt set per client, run on a schedule, so month-to-month numbers are comparable. Second, a one-page report: mention rate, average position in answers, top cited sources, and the two or three competitors who appear most often. Third, a short list of fixes: pages to improve, questions to answer on the site, and facts the AI models keep getting wrong. Fourth, a note explaining what changed and what happens next.

Resist the urge to track hundreds of prompts per client at the start. Five to ten well-chosen prompts per brand, covering the buying questions customers actually ask, produce a report a client can read. Fifty prompts produce a spreadsheet nobody opens.

## Pricing it without guessing

Price the service on the cost of the tracking plus your reporting time. The tracking itself is cheap: a tool like BrandSight AI runs 50 prompts for 5 brands daily on its $19 a month Pro plan, and its $49 a month Agency plan covers 20 brands with 200 prompts daily plus white-label reports. That means the tracking cost per client is a few dollars a month. The real cost is your time writing the report and the fixes, so price the retainer on that: a small monthly add-on to an existing SEO retainer, or a standalone package if the client buys GEO alone.

## Reporting that keeps clients

The report is the retention tool. Send it on the same day every month, keep the format identical, and lead with the change since last month, not the raw numbers. Clients stay when they can see movement: a mention rate that rose, a competitor that fell behind them, a citation from a page you fixed last month. When the numbers do not move, say so and say what you are changing. Honest flat months keep more clients than polished excuses.

Start with one or two clients, run the routine for a month, and then make it a standard offer. Your clients are already asking what AI says about them. The agency that answers first wins the work.
