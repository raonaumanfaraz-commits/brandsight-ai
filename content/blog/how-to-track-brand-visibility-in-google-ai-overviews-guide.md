Google AI Overviews sit at the top of Google Search and answer the question directly, often before anyone scrolls to the blue links. When someone searches for the best tool, agency, or product in your category, an AI Overview either names your brand or it does not. There is no brand dashboard for AI Overviews and no way to submit your brand for a mention, so tracking visibility there is a manual routine. Here is a method that takes about thirty minutes a week.

## Step 1: Fix your prompt list

Pick ten to fifteen questions your buyers actually type into Google, mixing three kinds: direct comparisons ("best AI visibility tool for small agencies"), problem questions ("how do I track brand mentions in AI answers"), and category questions ("AI search analytics tools for SEO teams"). Write them down exactly as a buyer would type them, and keep the wording fixed week to week, or you cannot compare weeks.

Include your brand name in only two or three prompts; the rest should be brand-free, because that is how buyers search before they know you exist.

## Step 2: Ask Google the same way every week

Once a week, run every prompt in a signed-out browser window, with the same location setting each time. AI Overviews are personalised and location-sensitive, so a signed-in search from your office will not match what a buyer sees. Note four things for each prompt: whether an AI Overview appeared at all, whether your brand was named in it, which sources it linked to, and where your brand sat in the answer, first, mentioned in passing, or not at all. Take a screenshot of every overview that mentions you; answers change without warning, and a screenshot is the only record you can trust later.

## Step 3: Read the citations, not just the answer

The links inside an AI Overview matter more than the words. If your brand is named but the citation points to a competitor's blog or a directory listing, Google is trusting someone else's page about you, not yours. If your own page is cited, check which page it is: a pricing page, a comparison post, or a guide. Over a month, the pattern tells you which pages Google treats as your authority, and which pages need a clearer, more factual rewrite.

## Step 4: Check Search Console for the traffic side

Search Console does not give you a separate AI Overviews report, so do not go looking for one. What it does show is the traffic side of the same story: clicks and impressions for the queries on your list, counted as ordinary web search. If an overview starts naming you and clicks on those queries rise in the same weeks, the two are probably connected. If impressions rise but clicks fall, the overview may be answering the question so fully that fewer people click through, which is a real trade-off worth knowing about.

## Step 5: Keep a simple weekly sheet

One row per prompt per week, with columns for overview shown, brand named, position, and cited source. After four weeks you will see the real signal: which prompts you appear in consistently, which ones flicker, and which ones never show you. That is the list to act on. For the prompts where you never appear, the fix is usually content: a page that answers that exact question with facts, numbers, and a clear comparison, written so an AI can quote it cleanly.
