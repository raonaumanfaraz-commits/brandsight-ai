Getting cited by an AI assistant is not luck. ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity quote pages that answer questions directly, back claims with evidence and stay easy to read. This checklist covers the twelve things every page needs before it has a real chance of being cited.

## 1. One clear question per page

Every page should answer one specific question a real person would ask an AI assistant. If your page tries to answer five questions at once, the AI will pick a sharper page instead. Start with the question in your heading and answer it in the first paragraph.

## 2. A direct answer in the first 50 words

AI engines lift short, self-contained answers. Put the core answer at the top, in plain language, before any background. A reader who stops after one paragraph should still leave with the answer.

## 3. Headings that read like questions

Use headings phrased as the questions people actually type, such as "How much does it cost?" rather than "Pricing overview". Question-style headings help AI models match your page to real prompts.

## 4. Short paragraphs and lists

Dense walls of text are hard for AI to quote cleanly. Keep paragraphs to two or three sentences and use bullet lists for steps, features and comparisons. A list item is far more likely to be cited than a buried sentence.

## 5. Real numbers, not vague claims

Cite specific figures, dates and prices where they matter. "Pro costs $19 a month" is quotable; "affordable pricing" is not. Check every number against the source before you publish, and update it when it changes.

## 6. Named sources and links

Link to the original source of any fact you did not produce yourself. AI engines trust pages that show their work, and a link to a primary source is a strong signal that your content is grounded.

## 7. Your own data or experience

Original findings, test results and first-hand experience are the hardest content for competitors to copy and the easiest for AI to cite as unique. Even a small survey or a before-and-after test counts.

## 8. A named author and date

Pages with a visible author and a recent publication or update date look more trustworthy to both readers and AI systems. Review older pages regularly and refresh the date only when you have actually updated the content.

## 9. A comparison or verdict

When people ask AI assistants to choose between options, the models quote pages that compare them clearly. A simple table of prices, features or trade-offs gives the model something concrete to cite.

## 10. Plain language, no jargon

Write the way your customers talk, not the way your industry talks. AI models match everyday phrasing far more often than internal jargon, so simple wording wins more citations.

## 11. A FAQ section for follow-up questions

Add three or four short questions and answers at the end of the page. These cover the follow-up prompts people ask after the main question and give AI engines more quotable material from your page.

## 12. A check after publishing

Publishing is only half the job. Ask an AI assistant the question your page answers and see whether your brand or page is mentioned. If it is not, work through this checklist again and fix the weakest point first, then recheck a week later.

## How to use this checklist

Run through all twelve points before you hit publish, and again when a page stops getting cited. Most pages fail on only two or three items, usually the direct answer at the top and the lack of real numbers. Fix those first and the rest follows.
