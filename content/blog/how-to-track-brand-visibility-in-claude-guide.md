Claude is now one of the main places people ask for product recommendations, yet it has no brand dashboard. Unlike Google Search Console, there is no official report that tells you how often Claude mentions your brand. That does not mean you cannot measure it. With a repeatable prompt set and a simple log, you can track your visibility in Claude week by week.

## Start with the questions buyers actually ask

Visibility only matters for the questions your buyers type. Write down 10 to 20 real buyer questions: "best CRM for a small agency", "top AI visibility tools", "alternatives to [competitor]". Include comparison questions, best-of lists and problem questions such as "how do I track brand mentions in AI answers". Keep the list fixed for at least a month, because changing the questions every week makes the trend meaningless.

## Run the same prompts every week

Open Claude and ask each question in a fresh conversation, so earlier answers do not colour the results. For every question, record four things: whether your brand is mentioned, whether it is recommended or merely listed, whether a link or source is cited, and which competitors appear instead. A simple spreadsheet with one row per prompt and one column per week is enough. Run the set on the same day each week, and run each prompt twice, because answers vary between runs.

## Score mentions, citations and position

A mention is not the same as a recommendation. Give each answer a simple score: 0 if your brand is absent, 1 if it is mentioned in passing, 2 if it is recommended, and 3 if it is the top recommendation with a citation. Track your average score per week and your share of voice, which is your mentions divided by all brand mentions across the same prompts. A citation matters most, because Claude's cited sources are the pages its answer is built on, and those pages are usually the ones you can improve.

## Check what Claude can actually see

Claude can only cite pages it can reach. Check that your key pages are not blocked by robots.txt or a firewall, that they load quickly, and that they answer buyer questions in plain language. Pages with clear headings, comparison tables and up-to-date facts are easier for an AI engine to quote. If a competitor keeps getting cited for a question you should win, open the cited page and see what it says that yours does not. The fix is usually a content gap, not a tracking problem.

## Use a tracker that covers Claude

Manual checks work for a small prompt set, but they do not scale. Some AI visibility tools now include Claude. Citlyze offers Claude as a paid add-on at $29 a month for up to 25 prompts a day. When you compare tools, ask three questions: how many prompts a day are covered in Claude, whether the tool shows the cited sources, and whether results are sampled more than once per prompt. A single daily check can mislead, because answers change between runs.

## Act on what you find

Tracking is only useful if it changes what you publish. Each week, pick the one prompt where your score dropped or a competitor gained ground, and fix the underlying page: add the missing fact, publish the comparison, or update an out-of-date claim. Then watch that prompt for two more weeks. If the score recovers, the fix worked. If it does not, the problem may be that the cited source is a review site or a directory, in which case getting listed there matters more than editing your own page.

Claude is becoming a recommendation engine, and the brands that measure their presence in it will be the ones that improve it.
