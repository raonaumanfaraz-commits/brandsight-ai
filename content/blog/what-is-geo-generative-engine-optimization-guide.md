Search used to end with a list of links. Increasingly, it ends with an answer. When someone asks ChatGPT, Gemini, or DeepSeek for the best tool, the best agency, or the best product in a category, the AI writes a short answer, names a few brands, and sometimes links to sources. GEO, short for generative engine optimization, is the work of making your brand one of the brands that answer names, and one of the sources it cites.

## How GEO differs from SEO

SEO optimizes for a ranked list of pages. The goal is a higher position on a search results page, and the reward is a click. GEO optimizes for a synthesized answer. The goal is to be mentioned in the answer itself, with a citation where possible, because many people read the answer and never click further.

That difference changes what counts as success. A page can rank well on Google and still never appear in an AI answer, because the AI draws on different signals: how clearly a brand is described, how often it is mentioned across trusted sources, and whether its claims are easy to verify.

## How AI answers choose brands

AI search tools do not browse the web the way a person does. They combine what they learned during training with fresh information retrieved at answer time, then summarize. Three things tend to help a brand get picked:

1. **Clear positioning.** Pages that state, in plain words, what the product does, who it is for, and how it differs from alternatives are easier for a model to summarize accurately.
2. **Consistent mentions.** The same description, repeated across your site, directories, reviews, and press coverage, gives the model a stable picture of your brand.
3. **Citable facts.** Pricing, features, and specifications stated on your own pages give the model something concrete to cite, rather than a vague claim it must paraphrase.

None of this is a trick. Models favour brands they can describe confidently. Vague copy, contradictory pricing, and missing product details all make a brand harder to mention.

## A simple GEO starting routine

You do not need a large budget to start. A practical weekly routine looks like this:

## Check what AI already says about you

Run the same five to ten questions your customers ask, across ChatGPT, Gemini, and DeepSeek, and record which brands appear, where yours ranks, and whether you are cited. This is your baseline. Without it, every later change is guesswork.

## Fix your own pages first

Make sure your homepage and product pages answer the obvious questions: what it is, who it is for, what it costs, and what makes it different. Add a short FAQ section that answers real customer questions in full sentences, since AI answers are built from sentences, not keywords alone.

## Build mentions beyond your site

Listings, directories, reviews, and guest articles all feed the sources models retrieve. Keep your description consistent across them. One clear, repeated description beats ten different taglines.

## Track changes weekly

AI answers change as models update and new content appears. Re-run your prompt set weekly and note movement: new mentions, lost mentions, and new competitors. A brand that is tracked is a brand that can be improved.

## What GEO cannot do

GEO cannot make a weak product look strong for long. If customers consistently complain about the same problem, AI answers will eventually reflect that too. It also cannot replace a good website or good service: the answer is only the front door. And it is not instant. Mentions shift over weeks as new content is published, not overnight.

The opportunity is real, though. The brands that appear in AI answers today are the ones that made themselves easy to describe, easy to verify, and easy to cite.
