ChatGPT is where a growing share of buyers now start their research. Instead of scanning ten blue links, they ask a question and get one answer. If your brand is in that answer, you are in the running. If it is not, you never existed for that buyer. Tracking your visibility in ChatGPT is how you find out which side of that line you are on, and what to do about it.

## Start with the prompts your buyers actually ask

Visibility only means something against the right prompts. List the questions a buyer asks before choosing a product like yours: "best CRM for a small law firm", "cheapest invoicing tool for freelancers", "which project management app works offline". Aim for 20 to 50 prompts, and mix three kinds: direct comparisons ("X vs Y"), category questions ("best tool for..."), and problem questions ("how do I automate invoices").

Use the words buyers use, not your marketing copy. Check your search console queries, your sales calls, and the questions people ask in forums in your niche. A prompt list built from real buyer language will tell you far more than one built from your own website headings.

## Check the answers by hand first

Before buying any tool, run the check manually once. Open a fresh ChatGPT session, ask each prompt, and record three things for every answer: whether your brand is mentioned at all, where it appears (first recommendation, middle of a list, or a footnote), and which sources are cited. Also note which competitors are named, and whether the answer recommends them more strongly than you.

Do this in a simple spreadsheet with one row per prompt. Repeat it once a week with the same prompts, in fresh sessions, so your history does not skew the results. Two or three weeks of manual checks will show you the actual wording buyers see.

## Measure four numbers

Four metrics cover almost everything that matters. Mention rate is the share of your prompts where your brand appears at all: if you are named in 6 of 30 answers, your mention rate is 20 percent. Average position is where you sit when you are mentioned: first, second, or buried at the end. Citation rate is how often the answer links to one of your pages, which is what turns a mention into traffic. Share of voice is your mentions measured against your competitors across the same prompt set.

Track all four weekly. Mention rate tells you whether you are visible, position tells you whether you are winning, citations tell you whether visibility becomes clicks, and share of voice tells you whether you are gaining on competitors.

## Turn the findings into fixes

A visibility check is only useful if it changes something. When an answer recommends a competitor, read the sources it cites: those pages are winning the citation, and you need to understand why. Usually the cited page answers the question more directly, has clearer comparison data, or is simply newer.

When your brand is mentioned but never cited, the fix is usually on your own pages: add the facts the answer is missing, such as pricing or a comparison table. When your brand is absent entirely, check whether your content covers the prompt's topic at all. Fix one gap per week and re-check; small, steady changes compound.

## Build a weekly routine

Set a fixed day and keep the routine short. Run your prompt list, update the four numbers, note the biggest change since last week, and pick one fix. That is the whole routine, and it takes under an hour once the prompt list exists. What matters is doing it every week with the same prompts, because AI answers shift as models update and as competitors publish new content.
