For years, ranking on Google was the whole game. If your page sat at position one, buyers found you. AI search changed the rules. ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity do not show a ranked list of ten links. They write an answer, name a few brands, and cite a few sources. In that world, a brand mention and a ranking are two different things, and you need to track both.

## What a ranking measures

A ranking measures where your page appears in a search results page for a specific keyword. It is public, repeatable, and tied to one page on your site. Rank trackers report it daily, and SEO work is built around moving it up.

Rankings still matter. Google still sends most website traffic, and AI Overviews still draw on pages that rank well. A strong ranking usually means your content is trusted, well structured, and relevant to the query.

But a ranking says nothing about what an AI assistant tells a buyer. A page can rank third and never be named in a ChatGPT answer, while a competitor ranked eighth gets recommended by name.

## What a brand mention measures

A brand mention in AI search measures whether an assistant names your brand when a buyer asks a question, and in what context. It is not tied to a single page. The assistant may mention you because of a review, a comparison article, a forum thread, or your own site.

Mentions also carry position and tone. Being named first in a shortlist is different from being mentioned last with a caveat. Being cited as the source is different from being named without a link. And being recommended is different from being mentioned as an example of what not to buy.

This is why counting mentions alone is not enough. A useful mention report shows the prompt that triggered it, where your brand appeared in the answer, whether your site was cited, and how often the same result repeats across runs. AI answers change between runs, so one mention is a data point, not a verdict. A pattern across weeks is the signal.

## Why the two diverge

Rankings and mentions diverge for a simple reason: they are built from different evidence. Google ranks pages. AI assistants synthesise answers from many sources at once, and they weigh clarity, consensus across sources, and how directly a source answers the question.

That is why a brand with strong reviews, clear comparison pages, and mentions across independent sites can win AI answers without winning the top ranking. And it is why a brand that ranks well for a keyword can still lose the AI answer if its content is thin, or if competitors are cited more often.

## How to track both without doubling your work

Start with the questions your buyers actually ask, not your keyword list. Write ten to twenty prompts the way a buyer would ask them: "best CRM for a small agency", "which invoicing tool is easiest to set up", "alternatives to X for freelancers". Run the same prompts every week on ChatGPT and Gemini, and record whether your brand is named, where it appears, and which pages are cited.

Then match that against your rankings for the same topics. Four combinations tell you what to do next. High ranking and high mentions means your content is working in both worlds, so protect it. High ranking and low mentions means your pages rank but are not being picked as an answer, so check whether your content states a clear recommendation or comparison. Low ranking and high mentions means other sources are carrying you, so build your own pages on those topics. Low on both means the topic needs fresh content and fresh citations.

One more habit pays off: track competitors the same way. If a rival is mentioned in answers where you are not, look at what sources the assistant cites for them. The fix is usually a content gap, not a ranking gap.

## The metric that matters

Rankings tell you where your pages sit. Mentions tell you whether buyers hear your name when they ask AI for help. In AI search, the mention is the outcome, and the ranking is only one of the ways to earn it. Track both, but judge your visibility by the answer, not the list.
