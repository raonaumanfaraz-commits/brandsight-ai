When ChatGPT or Gemini answers a question about your brand, it usually names its sources. Those citations are the pages the model trusts. A citation audit is a simple, repeatable check: run the questions your customers ask, write down every citation, and see whether your pages appear, your competitors' pages appear, or nobody is cited at all. The result tells you exactly which pages to fix first.

## Start with the questions customers actually ask

Pick ten to twenty prompts covering how customers really ask. Include direct brand prompts ("What is BrandSight AI?"), category prompts ("What is the best AI visibility tool for a small agency?"), and comparison prompts ("BrandSight vs Brand24"). Write them down word for word and do not rewrite them between runs, because changing the wording means you are testing a new question, not measuring a trend.

## Run them on every model you care about

Run the same set on ChatGPT, Gemini, and DeepSeek on the same day, and record the date and the model with each answer. AI answers vary from run to run, so one answer is a snapshot, not a verdict. Repeating the same set weekly shows whether a brand appears in most runs or never appears at all, and that pattern is what matters.

## Record every citation, not just the mentions

For each prompt, record five things: whether your brand was mentioned, where it appeared in the answer, which sources were cited, and the date and model of the run. Then classify every cited source into one of four groups: your own site, a competitor's site, a review site or directory, or news and other pages. Count how often each domain appears across the whole set. A competitor cited in half your prompts is a bigger signal than a single mention of your brand.

## Read the results as a to-do list

If your pages are never cited, you have a content gap: no clear page answers that question, so publish one that does, with facts, pricing, and a comparison the model can quote. If competitors are cited instead of you, study what their page does better, then match it with your own facts rather than copying their words. If you are cited with wrong facts, fix the source page first, because the model repeats what the page says. If no sources are cited at all, the model is answering from general knowledge, and a well-structured page can still earn a citation on the next run.

## Repeat monthly and keep the log

Run the same prompt set once a month and keep every result in one sheet. Track which citations appear and disappear, and make one content change at a time so you can see what moved the result. Over three or four months the log becomes a record of which pages earn trust and which questions still belong to your competitors, and that is the whole point of the audit: fewer guesses, and a fix list written by the models themselves.
