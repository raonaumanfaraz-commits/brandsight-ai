Google AI Mode is the part of Google Search that answers a question with a generated summary, often before the familiar list of blue links. When a potential customer asks for the best tool, service, or supplier in your category, AI Mode may name a few brands and never show the rest. If your brand is missing from those answers, you are invisible at the exact moment a buying decision forms.

## What AI Mode actually shows

AI Mode sits on top of Google Search and builds its answers from the same web, but it reads pages the way a language model reads them. It favours clear, factual, well-structured pages and it cites its sources. That means visibility in AI Mode depends less on ranking first and more on being the source the model trusts enough to quote.

The answers are conversational, so the same question asked two different ways can produce two different brand lists. Tracking one keyword is not enough. You need a small set of prompts that mirror how real customers ask.

## Step 1: Build your prompt list

Start with ten to fifteen prompts in the words your customers actually use. Mix three kinds: direct recommendations ("best AI visibility tool for agencies"), comparisons ("BrandSight vs alternatives"), and problem questions ("how do I know if ChatGPT mentions my brand"). Add your brand name to a few of them ("what does BrandSight do") and leave it out of the rest, so you can see whether the model mentions you unprompted.

Write the prompts down in a simple list and keep the wording fixed. Changing the wording every week makes the results impossible to compare.

## Step 2: Check AI Mode manually once a week

For each prompt, open Google Search, switch to AI Mode, and record three things: whether your brand is mentioned, whether you are cited with a link, and which competitors are mentioned instead. A short spreadsheet works fine for this.

## Step 3: Automate the daily tracking

A visibility tracker automates the same routine. It runs your prompt list every day, records whether your brand appears, whether it is cited, and which competitors show up, then shows the trend over time. That trend is the important part. A single day's result is noise, but a rising or falling line over four weeks tells you whether your content work is paying off.

When choosing a tracker, check that it covers the engines your customers actually use. Some tools cover only ChatGPT, while others cover Google AI Overviews and AI Mode as well. Also check whether it reports citations, not just mentions, because a mention without a link sends you no traffic.

## Step 4: Read the trend, then fix the gaps

Once you have two or three weeks of data, look for patterns. If your brand appears in comparison prompts but never in recommendation prompts, your content probably explains your features but never positions you as a top choice. If you are mentioned but never cited, your pages may lack the clear facts and structure that models quote. If one competitor appears in almost every answer, study their content: their pages are probably the model's favourite source, and matching their clarity is the fastest way to close the gap.

Fix one gap at a time. Add a clear comparison page, tighten a product page with facts and numbers, or publish a guide that answers the exact question the model keeps answering without you. Then watch the next two weeks of data.

## Keep it simple and keep it regular

Tracking visibility in Google AI Mode does not need a big budget or a complicated setup. A fixed prompt list, a weekly manual check, and a daily automated tracker will show you exactly where you stand, and the trend will tell you whether your work is paying off.
