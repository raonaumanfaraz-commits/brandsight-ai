Microsoft Copilot answers millions of questions every day, drawing on Bing search and the web. When someone asks it for the best tool, agency, or product in your category, your brand either appears in that answer or it does not. Copilot has no brand dashboard, so tracking visibility there is a manual routine. Here is a practical method that takes about thirty minutes a week.

## Step 1: Fix your prompt list

Pick ten to fifteen questions your buyers actually ask. Mix three kinds: direct comparisons ("best AI visibility tool for small agencies"), problem questions ("how do I track brand mentions in AI answers"), and category questions ("AI search analytics tools for SEO teams"). Write them down exactly as a buyer would type them, and do not change the wording week to week. Changing the prompt changes the answer, and then you cannot compare weeks.

Include your brand name in only two or three of the prompts. The rest should be brand-free, because that is how buyers search before they know you exist.

## Step 2: Ask Copilot the same way every week

Once a week, open Copilot in a fresh session and run the full list, one prompt at a time. Use the same entry point each week, such as copilot.microsoft.com or the Edge sidebar, and stay signed out or use the same account, because personalisation changes answers. Do not click any result while you work through the list, or later answers may shift.

For each prompt, record four things in a sheet: whether your brand was mentioned at all, where in the answer it appeared, which sources Copilot cited, and whether a competitor was recommended instead. That is the whole dataset. Mention rate, position, and citations are the three numbers that matter.

## Step 3: Read the citations, not just the mention

A mention without a citation is weak visibility. Copilot shows its sources, so open the cited pages and check two things: is the page yours, and does it actually answer the question? If Copilot cites a competitor's page for your category, that page is the one to beat. If it cites a review roundup that leaves you out, getting listed there is the fix.

Also note when Copilot's answer is wrong or outdated about your brand. Wrong answers are common with new tools and new pricing, and they are worth fixing at the source page first, because Copilot will keep repeating what its sources say.

## Step 4: Compare week to week

After four weeks you have a trend instead of a snapshot. Look for three patterns: prompts where you never appear, prompts where you appear but below a competitor, and prompts where you appeared and then disappeared. The first group needs new content, the second needs stronger pages, and the third usually means a source page changed or a competitor published something better.

Do not react to a single week. AI answers vary run to run, so only a change that holds for two weeks in a row is worth acting on.
