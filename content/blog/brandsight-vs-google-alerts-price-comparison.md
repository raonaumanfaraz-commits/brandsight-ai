## The prices, side by side

Google Alerts has no paid plan. It is free to use with a Google account, and its page lists no pricing tiers, no premium upgrade, and no paid add-ons. You create an alert for a keyword, choose a source type such as news, blogs, web, video, books, or discussions, pick how often you want results, and Google emails matching pages to you.

BrandSight's pricing is published on its own pricing page. The Free tier costs $0 a month and tracks 1 brand with 5 search prompts and weekly auto-checks across OpenAI and Google Gemini. Pro costs $19 a month and tracks 5 brands with 50 search prompts and daily auto-checks across OpenAI, Gemini, and DeepSeek, plus competitor share-of-voice alerts. The Agency plan costs $49 a month and tracks 20 brands with 200 search prompts and daily auto-checks, plus white-label reports and data export.

## What free alerts cover, and what they miss

Google Alerts watches the indexed web. If a news site, blog, or forum publishes a page that mentions your brand name, you get an email with a link. That is genuinely useful for reputation monitoring, and for a small brand with a low mention volume it may be all you need. It costs nothing, takes minutes to set up, and needs no software.

What it does not do is just as important. Google Alerts does not measure sentiment, does not count share of voice against competitors, and does not keep a history you can chart over time. It also does not see AI answers at all. When someone asks ChatGPT or Gemini for a product recommendation, no web page is published, so no alert fires, even if your brand is never mentioned in the answer. The conversation that shapes the buying decision happens entirely outside the indexed web that Google Alerts watches.

## When paying for AI visibility tracking makes sense

The gap matters most when customers discover products through AI answers rather than search results. If your buyers ask an assistant which tool, agency, or service to choose, your visibility in those answers is a marketing channel, and free alerts give you no read on it. BrandSight's Free tier is the low-risk way to check: it tracks one brand with five prompts on a weekly cycle at no cost, so you can see whether AI assistants mention you before spending anything.

Pro at $19 a month fits a growing brand or creator that wants daily checks across five brands and fifty prompts, with alerts when a competitor's share of voice moves. Agency at $49 a month fits consultancies managing many client brands, with white-label reports they can hand to clients. Neither tier replaces Google Alerts: the two tools watch different places, and many teams sensibly run both, free alerts for the indexed web and AI tracking for assistant answers.

## The honest bottom line

If your only question is "did anyone publish a page about us this week," Google Alerts answers it for free, and paying for anything else would be wasteful. If your question is "do AI assistants recommend us, and how do we compare with competitors in their answers," free alerts cannot answer it, because those answers are never published as pages. That is the line between the two products: one monitors what the web publishes, the other monitors what AI assistants say.
