AI search is now part of how customers find brands. When someone asks ChatGPT or Gemini for a recommendation, the answer comes from what those models know, and that knowledge changes as new content appears. A weekly check keeps you from discovering a problem months after it started. This routine takes about 30 minutes and works for any brand, product, or agency client.

## Pick your prompt set and freeze it

Start with 10 to 20 prompts your customers actually ask. Include direct questions ("What is the best invoicing tool for freelancers?"), comparison questions ("BrandSight vs Brand24"), and problem questions ("How do I track brand mentions in AI search?"). Write them down and use the exact same wording every week. If you change the prompts, you lose the trend line, and the trend is the whole point.

Add two or three competitor prompts too, so you can see whether a rival is gaining ground while you stand still.

## Run the checks on the same day each week

Run the same prompts across ChatGPT, Gemini, and DeepSeek on the same day each week, and record three things for each answer: whether your brand is mentioned, whether the mention is positive or neutral, and whether a source or citation links back to you. A simple spreadsheet is enough. Tools like BrandSight automate this, running the prompts daily and logging mentions and citations for you, but the discipline matters more than the tool.

## Read the week-over-week change, not the absolute number

One week of results tells you almost nothing. Four weeks tells you a story. Look for movement: a brand that appeared in 6 of 10 answers last week and 3 of 10 this week has a problem worth investigating. A new competitor appearing in answers where it never appeared before is a signal to check what changed on their side, a new page, a new review, or a new listing.

Do not panic over a single bad week. AI answers vary from run to run. Two or three weeks in the same direction is a trend, and trends are what you act on.

## Turn gaps into a short fix list

Every week, pick the single biggest gap and write one fix for it. If your brand is missing from comparison answers, publish or improve a comparison page. If answers cite a competitor's help center instead of yours, write the guide that answers the question better. If a review site outranks you in citations, ask happy customers to leave reviews there.

Keep the fix list to one or two items a week. GEO work compounds, and a steady stream of small, targeted pages beats a one-off content sprint every time.

## Report it in one paragraph

End each week with a short note: mention rate this week, mention rate last week, biggest mover, and the one fix you shipped. Share it with your team or your client. A one-paragraph weekly report builds the habit, proves the work is happening, and makes the monthly review easy, because the story is already written.

Consistency is the advantage. Most brands never check how AI answers talk about them at all. A 30-minute weekly routine puts you ahead of them, and the data it produces tells you exactly where to spend your content effort next.
