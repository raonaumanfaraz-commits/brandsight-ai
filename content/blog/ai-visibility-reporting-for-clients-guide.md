AI search is now a channel clients ask about, and agencies that track it need a report clients actually understand. A good monthly AI visibility report does three things: it shows where the client appears in AI answers, it shows whether that is improving, and it shows what happens next. Anything else is decoration.

## Start with the one number clients remember

Every report needs a headline metric a client can repeat in a meeting. Mention rate is the simplest: out of the prompts you track for the client, in how many did an AI answer mention the brand? Report it as a percentage, show last month next to this month, and say plainly whether it went up or down. One number, one comparison, no jargon.

Avoid opening with a score you invented. If your tool gives a visibility score, explain in one sentence what goes into it, or leave it off the first page. Clients trust numbers they can follow, not numbers they have to take on faith.

## Show the answers, not just the counts

A mention rate tells a client they appeared. The actual answer tells them how they appeared. Include two or three real AI answers from the month, quoted briefly, with the prompt that produced them. Pick one good mention and one where a competitor was named instead. Clients remember these examples long after they forget the charts, and the examples make the next section, the fixes, feel obvious.

Note the source the AI cited when it mentioned the client, too. If a blog post, a review page, or a directory listing keeps showing up as the citation, that page is doing real work and deserves to be in the report.

## Track competitors on the same prompts

A client does not care about visibility in the abstract. They care about visibility next to the names their buyers compare them with. Run the same prompt set against two or three named competitors and report the share of voice: what percentage of mentions went to the client versus each competitor. When a competitor gains ground, say which prompts drove it and what changed in their answers. That is the part of the report that usually starts a real conversation.

## End with three actions, not thirty

A report that ends with a long list of recommendations gets filed away. End with three actions, each with an owner and a date. Typical actions are concrete: publish one page that answers a prompt the client keeps losing, update a page the AI is citing with wrong facts, or add the client to a directory the AI keeps citing for competitors. Next month, the report opens by saying whether last month's three actions happened and what they changed.

## Keep the format boring and regular

Send the report on the same day every month, in the same format, with the same headline number in the same place. Clients compare months, and a format that changes every time makes comparison impossible. A short email with the headline number, the three example answers, the competitor share, and the three actions beats a twenty-page PDF every time.

AI visibility reporting is still new, and most clients have no idea what good looks like. An agency that reports clearly, monthly, and honestly about the misses as well as the wins becomes the one clients trust when budgets are decided. Start with one number, show real answers, compare against real competitors, and finish with three actions. That is the whole report.
